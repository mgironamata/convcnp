# neuralhydrology LSTM vs. ConvCNP on CAMELS-US

Head-to-head comparison of an LSTM baseline with a rewritten ConvCNP. Both models use the same data pipeline, the same evaluation protocol and the same metric code (`neuralhydrology` 1.13.0). The ConvCNP plugs into neuralhydrology as a custom model (`convcnp_nh/`) and fixes the problems listed in `../REVIEW.md`.

For the full story (every change since 2020, everything tried and dropped, and the complete LSTM comparison), see [`CHANGES_AND_RESULTS.md`](CHANGES_AND_RESULTS.md).

## 3. Experimental design

| config | periods (train / val / test) | notes |
|---|---|---|
| `lstm_kratzert2019` | WY2000–08 / WY1981–89 / WY1990–99 | Kratzert et al. 2019 (HESS) setup, Daymet. A sanity check against the published single-seed and ensemble numbers |
| `lstm_mres`, `convcnp_mres` | WY1981–95 / WY1991–95\* / WY1996–2010 | The MRes split, identical for both models |
| `convcnp_cmal_mres` | as above | Probabilistic ConvCNP (CMAL head, CMAL NLL) |
| `*_mres_tune` | WY1981–90 / WY1991–95 / (test = WY1991–95) | Hyper-parameter search without touching the test period (`experiments.sh tune`) |
| `convcnp_kratzert2019` | Kratzert periods | ConvCNP on the published setup |

\* In the `mres` configs the validation years overlap training. Validation is used for monitoring only; the last epoch is always evaluated.

Shared settings:
- 531 basins.
- Daymet `prcp, srad, tmax, tmin, vp`.
- The 27 static attributes of Kratzert et al. (2019).
- Target `QObs(mm/d)`.
- NSE\* loss (per-basin σ weighting).
- Adam with LR `{0: 1e-3, 10: 5e-4, 25: 1e-4}`, gradient clipping at 1, 30 epochs.

LSTM: `cudalstm`, 256 hidden units, `seq_length` 365, `predict_last_n` 1, dropout 0.4, batch 256 (Kratzert hyper-parameters, not re-tuned).

ConvCNP:
- 64 channels, 7 dilated blocks, kernel 3, receptive field 537 days, 236k parameters.
- `seq_length` 910 and `predict_last_n` 365: each window trains 365 targets, and each target sees its full 537-day history.
- Batch 64, 1000 updates per epoch.
- Causal, so no future forcing is ever used, the same information set as the LSTM.

**Evaluation protocol (both models):** every test day is predicted as the last step of its own window. For the ConvCNP this is exactly equivalent to training, because it is causal and `seq_length − predict_last_n + 1 ≥ receptive field` (checked at start-up; `--dry-run` prints it).

## Patches to neuralhydrology (applied by `register()`, covered by tests)

1. **Tester uses `predict_last_n = 1` for the ConvCNP.** With `predict_last_n = N`, neuralhydrology's tester only builds windows whose whole N-day block lies inside the period. It therefore silently skipped the first 364 days of validation and test (the test period would have started on 1996-09-29 instead of 1995-10-01). For a causal model the last-step prediction is unchanged by this.
2. **CMAL loss masks element-wise.** The stock `MaskedCMALLoss` drops every sample that has *any* missing target in its sequence, which gives NaN when a whole batch is dropped. The patched loss masks element-wise and takes the mean over valid points. For `predict_last_n = 1` it is identical to the original.

## Ideas once the baseline numbers exist

Roughly in order:
1. Seed ensembles. Kratzert et al. report 8-seed ensembles.
2. Tuning: width, depth and receptive field, dropout, static-MLP size (`experiments.sh tune`).
3. Probabilistic head (CMAL).
4. `input_dropout` (ConvCNP-style context subsampling as augmentation), then evaluate with *missing forcings*. Masking test inputs is where the density channel should beat an LSTM.
5. Assimilating observed discharge as an extra masked input channel (gap-filling / forecasting).
6. PUB via k-fold over basins.

Note: neuralhydrology evaluates one window per day. A causal ConvCNP could instead predict a whole basin in a single pass, which would make evaluation much faster.

## Results (2026-11-17)

MRes split, test WY1996–2010, 531 basins, Daymet. Summary CSV: `/scratch2/mg963/convcnp_runs/summary_final_mres.csv`.

| model | median NSE (seeds 1/2/3) | 3-seed ensemble median NSE | ensemble mean NSE | ensemble NSE < 0 | Alpha-NSE | FHV | train time per seed* |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LSTM (Kratzert hyper-parameters) | 0.736 / 0.740 / 0.740 | **0.764** | **0.719** | 3 | 0.94 | −5.7 | ~4.5 h |
| ConvCNP tuned (`convcnp_gs_mres`) | 0.725 / 0.721 / 0.722 | 0.752 | 0.709 | **2** | 0.88 | −12.5 | ~25 min |
| ConvCNP v1 (`convcnp_mres`) | 0.702 / 0.694 / 0.708 | 0.736 | 0.697 | 3 | 0.84 | −15.1 | ~45 min |
| SAC-SMA (calibrated per basin, mean of 10 seeds) | — | 0.665 | 0.572 | 11 | 0.87 | −10.7 | — |
| ConvCNP, 2020 MRes notebook | 0.37 | — | — | — | — | — | — |

\* Training time for a single job on the RTX 3090. Many runs shared the GPU.

**Findings.** Tuning was done on the tuning split (train WY1981–90, evaluate WY1991–95), with the test period untouched. Logs are in `convcnp_runs/logs/tune_*`.
- **Gave a clear gain:**
  - non-negative output (uncentred target + softplus). Removed clipped-zero low flows: FLV −144 → +14.
  - gated TCN blocks
  - more dropout plus no norm before the head
  - seed ensembling. Larger than usual for the ConvCNP, whose predictions are noisy between epochs.
- **Small or within seed noise (about ±0.007):** multi-scale encoder, 60-day target blocks with a bigger batch, half the training updates.
- **Hurt:**
  - input dropout (0.650). The model hedges, as in 2020.
  - 128 channels with less regularisation (0.705). Overfitting.
  - removing the in-block LayerNorm entirely. Collapsed to a constant output.

**Status.** The ConvCNP closed about half the gap. Remaining gap: single model −0.016, ensemble −0.012 median NSE. Its weakness is persistent over-smoothing: Alpha 0.88 vs 0.94, and peaks underestimated (FHV −12 vs −6). At equal or lower compute it wins: a 3-seed ConvCNP ensemble (about 1.3 GPU-hours) beats a single LSTM (about 4.5 GPU-hours), 0.752 vs 0.739.

## Prediction in ungauged basins (PUB), 2026-11-18

- **Setup:** 5-fold cross-validation over basins (`scripts/make_pub_folds.py`, seed 0; 106–107 test basins per fold). Training uses the fold's training basins over WY1981–95. Testing uses the held-out basins over WY1996–2010, so test basins are unseen in space and time. Results are pooled over folds, so each of the 531 basins is scored once, out-of-sample, by `scripts/pub_report.py`.
- **Models:**
  - ConvCNP: `convcnp_gss_pub_fold*`, which is `gs` plus `head_bias_init=1.0`.
  - LSTM: `lstm_pub_fold*`, with Kratzert hyper-parameters.

| model | median NSE | mean NSE | NSE < 0 | median KGE | Alpha-NSE | FHV |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LSTM, 1 seed per fold | **0.635** | 0.437 | 46 | **0.675** | 0.95 | −6.1 |
| ConvCNP, seed 1 / 2 / 3 | 0.626 / 0.630 / 0.616 | 0.446 / **0.465** / 0.458 | 37 / 38 / **36** | ~0.656 | 0.87 | −12.3 |
| ConvCNP, 3-seed ensemble | 0.664 | 0.519 | 33 | 0.675 | 0.85 | −13.7 |
| ConvCNP, 2020 MRes report (single 80/20 split) | 0.31 | — | — | — | — | — |

**How the ConvCNP compares with the LSTM (single seed vs single seed):**
- The median-NSE gap is −0.012 on average (gauged: −0.016).
- The ConvCNP is better in about 43% of basins.
- It has a higher mean NSE and fewer failing basins (36–38 vs 46).

**3-seed ensembles, like for like (2026-11-20).** LSTM seeds 2 and 3 were added for every fold. Summary: `convcnp_runs/pub_lstm3_vs_gss3_summary.csv`.

| model | median NSE | mean NSE | NSE < 0 | median KGE | Alpha-NSE | FHV |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LSTM, seed 1 / 2 / 3 | 0.635 / 0.644 / 0.644 | 0.437 / 0.452 / 0.324 | 46 / 38 / 48 | ~0.67 | 0.93 | −7 |
| LSTM, 3-seed ensemble | **0.679** | 0.478 | 37 | **0.694** | 0.91 | −8.5 |
| ConvCNP, 3-seed ensemble | 0.664 | **0.519** | **33** | 0.675 | 0.85 | −13.7 |

Paired by basin, the ConvCNP ensemble beats the LSTM ensemble in 40.7% of basins. The median per-basin difference is −0.014 (Wilcoxon p = 0.003). The LSTM therefore still wins on median NSE, by about the same margin as in the gauged setting. The ConvCNP is more robust in the tail: higher mean NSE and fewer failing basins.

**Stability note.** The tuned `gs` configuration had 3 of 15 PUB runs dead from epoch 1 (training loss stuck at about 0.1, constant output). `head_bias_init=1.0` fixed it: all 15 runs were healthy. Use the `gss` configs from now on.

## Over-smoothing pilots, 2026-11-20 (tuning split)

Baseline: `gss`, seeds 1/2/3 = 0.726/0.726/0.723, 3-seed ensemble 0.753. LSTM reference, single seed: 0.760. Logs: `convcnp_runs/logs/tune_L_*`.

- **`sharp`** (`decoder_scales [0.25]`, i.e. no 2-day read-out kernel). Seeds 0.736/0.737/0.725, ensemble 0.760.
  - The gain is +0.003 to +0.004 per basin for every seed, with win rates of 52–55% (ensemble p = 0.08).
  - Alpha is about +0.01 and FHV about +1.5.
  - The gain is small and consistent, but not significant.
- **`lowdrop`** (dropout 0.1 / 0.1): 0.707. Hurts.
- **`alpha_loss_weight` 0.3 / 1.0**: 0.697 / 0.636.
  - Alpha rises to 0.90 / 0.95 and FHV to −11.7 / −8.2, matching the LSTM.
  - But FLV drops to −26 / −64: the variance is inflated everywhere, not only at the peaks.
- **`alpha_loss_weight` 0.1**: dead start (constant output; the variance term has zero gradient at a flat output). Inconclusive.
- **Seed noise:** single-seed median NSE varies by about ±0.015 between configurations that are practically identical (`gs` vs `gss`, seed 1: 0.742 vs 0.726).
- **Mixed ensemble (PUB, no new training):** LSTM3 + ConvCNP3 gives median NSE 0.696, mean 0.537 and 29 basins with NSE < 0, against LSTM3 at 0.679.

**Batch N** (seed 1, compared with `gss` seed 1). The loss terms switch on after a 500-step warm-up on plain NSE\*.

| variant | median NSE | Alpha | FHV | FLV | basins better than `gss` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `peak_loss_weight` 0.5 (top 5% of flows per window) | 0.689 | 0.93 | −10.8 | −36 | 26% |
| `peak_loss_weight` 2.0 | 0.643 | 0.96 | −11.2 | −57 | 13% |
| `local_blocks` 2 | 0.732 | 0.87 | −14.7 | +4 | 48% |
| `local_blocks` 2 + `sharp` | 0.731 | 0.88 | −13.6 | +7 | 51% |
| `alpha_loss_weight` 0.1 + warm-up | 0.726 | 0.88 | −14.3 | −14 | 43% |

- **Warm-up fixes the dead start.**
- **Peak weighting behaves like the variance loss:** Alpha reaches the LSTM's level, but median NSE and low flows (FLV) degrade.
- **Local blocks are neutral.**

**Conclusion:** no loss or architecture change tried so far moves the ConvCNP's NSE–Alpha trade-off. The best ConvCNP is still `gss` or `sharp`.

## Gauged `gss` 6 seeds and missing forcings, 2026-11-23

Details: `CHANGES_AND_RESULTS.md` sections 3.4 and 3.5.
- **`gss` 6 seeds, gauged MRes:** median NSE 0.757, below LSTM 3 seeds (0.764) and 5 seeds (0.772). Still over-smoothed (Alpha 0.875). LSTM 5 + `gss` 6 mixed: 0.777.
- **Missing forcings, evaluation only** (`scripts/eval_missing_forcings.py`; models not trained with gaps). Median NSE, LSTM 5 vs `gss` 6, both with forward-filled gaps: 10% hidden 0.657 vs 0.653; 30% 0.347 vs 0.395 (`gss` better in 68% of basins); 50% −0.096 vs 0.057 (78%). Leaving gaps as NaN for the ConvCNP's density channel is worse than filling at every level, so that needs training with gaps.
